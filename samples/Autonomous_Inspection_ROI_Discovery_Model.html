What the inspection work costs, in their numbers.

Six answers given in a room, turned into one figure the customer will restate to their own director. Every field below is a question from the discovery call, not an assumption carried in from a benchmark.

This model prices one pool: displaced inspection labour. It is the conservative floor, chosen because it is the only pool that can be populated from answers given in a single conversation. Scaffolding avoidance, turnaround deferral, unplanned downtime and compliance exposure are deliberately unpriced — listed at the foot of this page with the reason for each.

The six questions

“How many things at this site actually get inspected, and how often?”

“When a tank roof needs inspecting, walk me through what has to happen. Who mobilises, how long does the permit take, what is standing by while it runs?”

“What does a resource on this work cost you fully loaded, and how many days a year are they actually available?”

“How many people would you say sit on this work today?” — asked last, and never used as an input to the cost.

Not priced here, and why

Value poolPricedReason for leaving it out
Scaffolding & rope access avoided—Contractor rates are site-specific and were not available. Typically the largest single omission.
Turnaround slot returned—Requires the site's own deferral economics. Cannot be estimated from outside.
Unplanned downtime avoided—Needs contribution margin per hour, which sits with finance rather than with the site.
Compliance & emissions exposure—Value is evidence quality under OGMP reporting, not cost avoided. Wrong unit for this model.
Safety exposure hours removed—Deliberately not monetised. Pricing exposure hours invites an argument the account does not need.

Each omission moves the case in one direction only. The figure above is therefore a floor, and it is quoted as a floor.

Prepared as an independent discovery instrument. No benchmark or vendor-supplied figures are used anywhere in this model — the defaults shown are placeholders to demonstrate structure and must be replaced with customer-supplied values before any figure is quoted. Where an input cannot be obtained in the call, the correct action is to leave the pool unpriced rather than to estimate it.
